RES-01 · Medical Imaging
Deepfake X-Rays Fool Radiologists 59% of the Time

A peer-reviewed study in the journal Radiology finds that AI-generated deepfake X-rays (synthetic medical images indistinguishable to the eye from genuine scans) are nearly impossible for trained radiologists to reliably detect. Across 264 images — half real, half AI-generated — radiologists identified fakes correctly only 41% of the time without warning, and only 62–78% even when explicitly told fakes were present, barely above guessing. For healthcare organizations and insurers, this is a concrete fraud vector: falsified scans could manipulate diagnoses, insurance claims, or clinical trial data at scale, and no commercial detection tool currently meets the threat.

RES-02 · Drug Discovery
Xaira Releases X-Cell: Largest Virtual Cell Model Ever Built

Biotech startup Xaira Therapeutics released X-Cell, a virtual cell model (an AI that simulates the internal machinery of biological cells rather than analyzing medical images or records) trained on 25.6 million cells across seven large-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screens — experiments that silence specific genes to measure downstream effects. The model uses a diffusion architecture (the same class of generative model powering image generators like Stable Diffusion, adapted here to generate molecular states) to predict how cells respond to genetic perturbations it has never seen before. Pharma R&D teams running lead optimization can now simulate thousands of gene-silencing experiments in silico, cutting wet-lab costs and timelines at the earliest, most expensive stage of drug discovery.
