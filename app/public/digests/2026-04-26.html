"Agentic World Modeling" Survey Unifies 400+ Works into Levels × Laws Taxonomy

Researchers published a comprehensive survey introducing a "levels × laws" taxonomy for world models in AI agents, synthesizing over 400 works across model-based reinforcement learning, video generation, web and GUI agents, multi-agent social simulation, and AI-driven scientific discovery — the first systematic attempt to define world modeling across AI subfields.

The framework defines three capability levels and four governing-law regimes that determine what constraints world models must satisfy and where they fail. It provides decision-centric evaluation principles and architectural guidance connecting previously isolated communities, charting a path from passive next-step prediction toward models that can simulate and reshape environments.

Built from nearly 10,000 real-world agents across multiple providers, this benchmark formalizes agent search as retrieval and reranking problems, revealing a consistent gap between semantic similarity and actual agent performance. Control-Theoretic Analysis Shows LLM Self-Correction Only Helps When Error-in-Refinement Rate Below 0.5%

Framing self-correction as a cybernetic feedback loop, researchers found a sharp near-zero EIR threshold separating beneficial from harmful iteration. Only o3-mini, Claude Opus 4.6, and o4-mini remained non-degrading; GPT-5 degraded by −1.8pp. A verify-first prompt ablation turned −6.2pp degradation into +0.2pp on GPT-4o-mini.

Study of 2M-Agent Platform Finds No Emergent Collective Intelligence — Interactions Remain Shallow

The first empirical evaluation of whether collective intelligence emerges spontaneously from scale studied MoltBook, a platform hosting over two million agents. Results showed the society failed to outperform individual frontier models on complex reasoning tasks, rarely synthesized distributed information, and often failed trivial coordination tasks.

Finding: Collective intelligence does not emerge from scale alone. Dominant limitation: sparse, shallow interaction — threads rarely extend beyond a single reply.

Microsoft PM Outlines AI Evaluation Stack: Deterministic and Model-Based Assertions for Enterprise LLM Quality

A Microsoft senior product manager published a comprehensive guide distinguishing Layer 1 deterministic assertions (schema validation, tool call correctness, JSON structure) from Layer 2 model-based assertions (LLM-as-a-Judge for semantic quality). The framework requires a state-of-the-art reasoning judge, a strict rubric defining failure gradients, and ground truth golden outputs — with offline pipelines requiring 95%+ pass rates on 200–500 test cases for enterprise-grade applications. The guide outlines a continuous improvement flywheel where production telemetry (retry rates, apology rates, explicit thumbs signals) feeds back into the golden dataset to prevent evaluation rot as user behavior evolves.
