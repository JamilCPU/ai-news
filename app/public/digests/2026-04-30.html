Bian Que: Agentic Framework for Online System Operations Reduces Alert Volume by 75% and Cuts MTTR by 50%

75% Alert reduction
80% RCA accuracy
>50% MTTR improvement
99% Offline pass rate

Researchers presented Bian Que, an agentic framework for operating and maintaining large-scale online engine systems — search, recommendation, advertising — that addresses the orchestration bottleneck in LLM-based agents. The system introduces Flexible Skill Arrangement, where each Skill specifies which data and knowledge to retrieve for a given business-module context and can be automatically generated and updated by LLMs or iteratively refined through natural-language instructions from on-call engineers. A unified self-evolving mechanism drives two parallel pathways from each correction signal: case-memory-to-knowledge distillation and targeted Skill refinement. Deployed on the e-commerce search engine of KuaiShou, China's major short-video platform, the results are concrete and production-verified.

SciHorizon-DataEVA: Agentic System for AI-Readiness Evaluation of Heterogeneous Scientific Data

Researchers proposed SciHorizon-DataEVA, a novel agentic system for scalable AI-readiness evaluation of heterogeneous scientific data. The Sci-TQA2 principles organize AI-readiness into four dimensions — Governance Trustworthiness, Data Quality, AI Compatibility, and Scientific Adaptability — decomposed into measurable atomic elements. A hierarchical multi-agent evaluation approach orchestrates through a directed, cyclic workflow that dynamically constructs dataset-aware evaluation specifications, enabling scalable assessment across heterogeneous scientific data.

XDFT: Self-Evolving Agent for Explainable Diagnosis of DFT-Experiment Band-Gap Mismatch

XDFT is a closed-loop agent that automatically diagnoses mismatches between density functional theory predictions and experimental results for electronic ground states of complex compounds. On a benchmark of 124 materials, XDFT identifies a resolving mechanism for 70 of 90 mismatch cases — 78% — an order of magnitude above both a uniform-random baseline (19%) and a static LLM ordering (20%). The system draws candidate hypotheses from a curated catalogue, executes first-principles tests, and updates a global Bayesian posterior over hypothesis usefulness from each verdict.

Atomic-Probe Governance for Skill Updates in Compositional Robot Policies

Researchers introduced Atomic-Probe Governance for skill updates in compositional robot policies, addressing the challenge that skill libraries in deployed robotic systems are continually updated yet existing typed-composition methods treat the library as frozen at test time. On a dual-arm peg-in-hole task they discovered a dominant-skill effect: one ECM achieves 86.7% atomic success rate while every other ECM sits at or below 26.7%, and whether this dominant ECM enters a composition shifts the success rate by up to +50pp. They propose an atomic-quality probe and a Hybrid Selector combining per-skill probes with selective composition revalidation.

Framework for Measurable Trust in Clinical AI: Evidence, Supervision, and Staged Autonomy

Researchers proposed a practical framework for trustworthy clinical AI built around three principles: evidence, supervision, and staged autonomy. Rather than replacing deterministic clinical logic wholesale with black-box models, the approach combines a deterministic core, a patient-specific AI assistant for contextual validation, a multi-tier model escalation mechanism, and a human supervision layer for verification and risk control. Trust metrics built on metrological principles — measurement uncertainty, calibration, traceability — enable quantitative rather than subjective assessment of each architectural layer.

Technical Note: Removing the ln ln T Term from the Squint Bound

Researchers presented a short technical note showing how to remove the ln ln T factor in the data-independent bound for the Squint algorithm. This is equivalent to changing the prior in the Krichevsky-Trofimov algorithm, applying the same idea used by Orabona and Pál [2016] to remove the ln ln T factor in the parameter-free learning with expert bound. A theoretical contribution to online learning and bandit algorithms, improving bounds for parameter-free learning.
