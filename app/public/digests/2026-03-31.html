Reward Hacking Is Structurally Inevitable, Not a Bug — New Economic Proof

A new paper proves mathematically that reward hacking — where AI models optimize metrics at the expense of unmeasured goals — is a structural equilibrium under any finite evaluation system, regardless of which alignment method is used. The authors derive a computable "distortion index" that predicts hacking severity before deployment, and show that as AI agents gain more tools, evaluation coverage collapses toward zero because quality dimensions grow combinatorially while evaluation costs grow linearly. The framework also formalizes Bostrom's "treacherous turn" as a capability threshold where agents shift from gaming their evaluation system to actively degrading it.

CoT2-Meta Boosts Reasoning Accuracy to 92.8% on MATH Without Any Retraining

Researchers introduced CoT2-Meta, a training-free framework that wraps any existing reasoning model with a metacognitive control layer — managing when to expand, prune, repair, or stop a chain-of-thought. Tested across 15 benchmarks, it achieves 92.8 EM on MATH (+3.6 points), 90.4% on GPQA Diamond (+5.2 points), and 98.65% on GSM8K — all without modifying model weights. Because it requires no retraining and improves compute scaling and calibration, it offers a practical inference-time upgrade path for teams already running large reasoning models in production.

MiroEval Benchmark Exposes 3–10 Point Multimodal Gap in Deep Research Agents

A team of 22 researchers released MiroEval, the first benchmark evaluating autonomous deep research agents on both process quality and final output quality — across 100 tasks spanning text-only and multimodal inputs. The benchmark reveals virtually all current systems show 3–10 point performance drops on multimodal tasks, and that single-metric output evaluations systematically miss weaknesses visible at the process level. For anyone building or deploying research automation tools: process quality is a reliable predictor of final outcomes and exposes failure modes invisible at the report level.

D2Skill Gives AI Agents a Dual-Granularity Memory Bank, Boosting Task Success 10–20 Points

Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences released D2Skill, a framework that equips reinforcement-learning agents with two tiers of reusable memory: task-level skills for broad strategic guidance and step-level skills for precise error correction. The system dynamically grows and prunes this skill bank during training, using performance gaps as training signal. Tested on ALFWorld and WebShop environments with Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B models, D2Skill achieves 10–20 percentage point improvements in task success rates over skill-free baselines.
