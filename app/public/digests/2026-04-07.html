The Ideation Bottleneck: AI Research Papers Are Executed Well But Conceived Poorly

A study of 953 papers found the quality gap between AI and human research is overwhelmingly about idea generation (71%), not execution. Human papers scored 47.1% 'exceptional' on ideas vs. 16.5% for AI, while execution gaps were far smaller. AI papers show striking methodological homogeneity — 74% use difference-in-differences — and fewer than 1% matched median human performance on both dimensions simultaneously.

The Augmentation Trap: AI Productivity Gains Can Destroy the Expertise They Depend On

Experimental and theoretical evidence that AI productivity tools can leave workers worse off long-term — even when adoption is individually rational. The mechanism is 'cognitive offloading': AI handles tasks workers would otherwise practice, eroding underlying expertise. Workers with low expertise-dependence may permanently deskill; experienced workers are more insulated. Enterprises should treat skill preservation as an active design constraint.

The Compression Gap: Why Scaling Robot AI With a Better Camera Often Fails

A paper on vision-language-action models identifies a 'Compression Gap': scaling only works at the actual bottleneck. Upgrading the vision encoder improved performance by 21+ percentage points with continuous action representations, but yielded almost no benefit with discrete action tokenization — the bottleneck was the codebook's capacity, not the camera. For robotics teams evaluating compute budgets, this framework helps avoid expensive wrong-bottleneck investments.

Ten Binary Questions Bridge 23–72% of the Gap Between Small and Large Language Models

A new compression paper introduces a 'Question-Asking' protocol — inspired by Twenty Questions — where a small model iteratively queries a larger model with yes/no questions (1 bit per answer). With just 10 binary questions, the small model recovers 23–72% of the capability gap on standard benchmarks. The technique achieves compression ratios of 0.0006–0.004, more than 100x smaller than prior LLM-based methods, with applications in model distillation and distributed inference.

Japan's Robot Deployments Fill Jobs Nobody Wants — Not Jobs People Have

Japan is accelerating physical AI deployments into live commercial operations, but the dynamic is structurally different from Western displacement narratives: robots are filling chronically vacant positions caused by demographic decline and an aging population, not displacing employed workers.
