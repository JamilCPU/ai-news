OpenAI Restructures Microsoft Partnership: Non-Exclusive License, No Revenue Share, Multi-Cloud Flexibility

OpenAI announced an amended agreement with Microsoft that fundamentally restructures their partnership, making Microsoft's license to OpenAI IP non-exclusive through 2032 and eliminating Microsoft's revenue share payments. Under the new terms, Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner and products will ship first on Azure, but OpenAI can now serve all its products to customers across any cloud provider. Microsoft continues to participate in OpenAI's growth as a major shareholder, and both companies continue collaborating on scaling gigawatts of new datacenter capacity, next-generation silicon, and applying AI to advance cybersecurity.

OpenAI Open-Sources Symphony: Orchestration Spec That Turns Issue Trackers into Agent Control Planes

OpenAI open-sourced Symphony, an orchestration spec that turns project management boards like Linear into control planes for coding agents — treating every open task as an agent assignment, with agents running continuously and humans reviewing results. The system achieved a 500% increase in landed pull requests on some teams at OpenAI within the first three weeks by shifting from interactive coding sessions to ticket-level orchestration, broadening who can initiate work: product managers and designers can file feature requests and receive review packets with video walkthroughs.

NVIDIA and Siemens Release NV-Raw2Insights-US: Physics-Informed AI for Adaptive Ultrasound Imaging

NVIDIA and Siemens Healthineers released a reconstruction model that learns directly from raw ultrasound sensor data, generating patient-specific sound-speed maps for adaptive image focusing in a single AI pass — what previously required complex, time-consuming computation. The architecture uses NVIDIA Holoscan Sensor Bridge for high-bandwidth low-latency RDMA transfer to GPU, running accelerated inference on Blackwell-class GPUs and streaming personalized sound-speed estimates back to ultrasound scanners in real time.

Case-Specific Rubrics for Clinical AI Evaluation: LLM Rubrics Approximate Clinician Agreement at 1,000x Lower Cost

Researchers validated a case-specific, clinician-authored rubric methodology using 20 clinicians who authored 1,646 rubrics across 823 clinical cases in primary care, psychiatry, oncology, and behavioral health — achieving a median score gap of 82.9% between high- and low-quality outputs with high scoring stability. In later experiments, clinician-LLM ranking agreement matched or exceeded clinician-clinician agreement, supporting incorporation of LLM rubrics alongside clinician-authored ones at roughly 1,000 times lower cost.

Agent Viability Framework: Adaptive Runtime Governance for Autonomous AI Agents

Researchers proposed the Informational Viability Principle for governing autonomous AI agents whose behavior drifts as adversaries adapt and decision patterns shift without code change — framing governance as estimating a bound on unobserved risk and allowing an action only when capacity exceeds that bound by a safety margin. RiskGate instantiates the framework with KL divergence, segment-vs-rest z-tests, and sequential pattern matching, with a scalar Viability Index enabling first-order prediction to transform governance from reactive to predictive.

XGRAG: Graph-Native Framework for Explaining Knowledge Graph-Based RAG

Researchers introduced XGRAG, which generates causally grounded explanations for GraphRAG systems by employing graph-based perturbation strategies to quantify the contribution of individual graph components on model answers — a gap existing text-based XAI methods cannot address. Extensive experiments demonstrated a 14.81% improvement in explanation quality over the RAG-Ex baseline across NarrativeQA, FairyTaleQA, and TriviaQA, with explanations exhibiting strong correlation with graph centrality measures.

Monad-Based Categorical Semantics Unifies ULLER Neurosymbolic Framework

Researchers showed that the three pairwise independent semantics of ULLER — classical, fuzzy, and probabilistic — are all instances of one categorical framework based on monads, enabling modular addition of new semantics and systematic translations between them. As an example, they outline the addition of generalized quantification in Logic Tensor Networks to arbitrary domains by extending the Giry monad to probability spaces, with initial implementations published in Python and Haskell.
