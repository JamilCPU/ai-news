MonitorBench Finds Proprietary LLMs Have Weaker Reasoning Transparency Than Open Models

Researchers released MonitorBench, a 1,514-instance benchmark across 19 tasks testing whether LLM chain-of-thought reasoning actually reflects model decision-making. Key findings: proprietary models have unexpectedly weaker monitorability than open alternatives; there is an inverse relationship between model capability and reasoning transparency; and under stress conditions, monitorability drops up to 30 percentage points. For AI safety practitioners evaluating whether they can audit model decisions, this is a concrete warning: the reasoning chains that look coherent may not be the actual computation driving outputs.

"The Novelty Bottleneck": Framework Explains Why AI Augments Rather Than Eliminates Senior Knowledge Work

Researcher Jacky Liang published a theoretical framework introducing the "novelty bottleneck" — novel tasks requiring human judgment form an irreducible serial constraint, similar to Amdahl's Law in parallel computing. The model predicts that when novelty is high, human effort scales linearly with task volume regardless of AI capability improvements; when novelty is low, effort drops to constant. Empirical alignment with AI coding benchmarks and research productivity data supports the framework, providing a formal foundation for workforce planning decisions around AI adoption.
