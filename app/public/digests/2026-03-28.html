Mistral Voxtral TTS: Multilingual Voice Cloning from 3 Seconds of Audio
68.4%
Win rate over ElevenLabs Flash v2.5

Mistral released Voxtral, a TTS (Text-to-Speech) model that clones a target voice from as little as 3 seconds of reference audio and synthesizes natural-sounding speech across multiple languages. The architecture combines autoregressive generation (predicting tokens one-by-one for expressiveness) with flow-matching (a technique for smooth, high-fidelity audio rendering), using a custom Voxtral Codec tokenizer to compress audio efficiently. In blind evaluations by native speakers, Voxtral achieved a 68.4% win rate over ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 — the commercial benchmark for real-time voice cloning. Weights are released under CC BY-NC, giving developers and media companies a deployable, locally-run alternative to subscription-based voice APIs.

RC2: Reinforcement Learning via Cross-Modal Cycle Consistency Boosts Reasoning by up to 7.6 Points

RC2 is a training technique that improves multimodal AI reasoning (the ability to answer questions that require jointly understanding images and text) without requiring any additional hand-labeled data. It works by testing whether a model can perform "cycle consistency" — start with a visual question, translate it to text, answer it, then reconstruct the original question from the answer, checking that everything stays coherent. This loop generates a dense reward signal for RL (Reinforcement Learning — training that improves a model by rewarding good outputs rather than explicit labels), boosting multimodal reasoning benchmarks by up to 7.6 points. For AI teams constrained by data labeling budgets, it's a compelling path to better multimodal performance through smarter training rather than more annotation.

WriteBack-RAG: Treating the Knowledge Base as a Trainable Component Improves Every RAG Setting Tested

WriteBack-RAG challenges a foundational assumption in RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation — systems that answer questions by first searching an external knowledge base rather than relying solely on the model's training data): that the knowledge base is a fixed, static corpus. The paper shows that using labeled examples to identify high-quality retrieval cases and then distilling that content into compact, reindexed knowledge units — written back into the corpus — improves answer quality by an average of +2.14% across four RAG pipelines, six benchmarks, and two different underlying LLMs, without any change to the retrieval algorithm. For enterprises running RAG over large internal document stores, this is a low-overhead quality improvement: the knowledge base itself becomes a continuously improving asset rather than a static dump.

WildASR: Voice Agents Hallucinate Plausible but Unspoken Content Under Degraded Inputs

WildASR is a new diagnostic benchmark that stress-tests ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition — the technology that converts spoken audio into text, powering voice assistants and call-center AI) using real-world recordings across multiple languages, accents, noise environments, and demographics. Tested across seven leading ASR systems, the benchmark reveals a critical failure mode: when audio is partial or degraded, models don't simply fail to transcribe — they confidently hallucinate plausible words and sentences that were never spoken, inserting invented content into the transcript. For enterprises deploying voice agents in customer service, healthcare intake, or legal transcription, this hallucination behavior is a direct liability: AI-generated fabrications that sound real can corrupt decisions downstream without any visible error signal.
