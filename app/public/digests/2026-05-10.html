Intent-based chaos testing: designed for when AI behaves confidently and wrongly

Intent-based chaos testing emerged as a new methodology for validating AI systems that behave confidently but incorrectly — addressing the challenge of testing agentic AI where traditional unit tests fail to capture emergent behaviors. The approach focuses on testing the intent behind AI decisions rather than just the outputs.

Via VentureBeat ↗
Why it matters
  • Agentic AI can autonomously execute complex workflows — a confident wrong action compounds through the entire pipeline
  • Traditional unit tests validate outputs; chaos testing validates intent — whether the system understood what it was supposed to do
  • Most critical for high-stakes deployments: medical, financial, infrastructure, and legal AI agents

OncoAgent: Dual-tier multi-agent framework for privacy-preserving oncology clinical decision support

OncoAgent is an open-source, privacy-preserving clinical decision support system for oncology that combines a dual-tier fine-tuned LLM architecture with a state-of-the-art multi-agent LangGraph topology. Clinical queries are routed through an additive complexity scorer to either a 9B speed-optimized model (Tier 1) or a 27B deep-reasoning model (Tier 2) — both fine-tuned on 266,854 real and synthetic oncological cases. Sequence packing on AMD Instinct MI300X completed full-dataset fine-tuning in approximately 50 minutes — a 56× throughput acceleration over API-based generation. The entire system is deployable on-premises with no proprietary cloud API dependency.
